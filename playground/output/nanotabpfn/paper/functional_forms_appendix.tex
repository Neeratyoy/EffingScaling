\section{Functional forms: details}
\label{app:functional-forms}

\paragraph{Notation.}
$C$ is training compute in FLOPs, $c=\ln(C/10^{13})$, $g=1-\text{AUC}$ the gap to a perfect ROC AUC, $g_0=0.5$ the gap
of a random classifier, and $\sigma$ the logistic function. Every form except the two references has an irreducible
gap $E$, so that its ceiling is $1-E$.

The reduced two-break BNSL variants set $c_0=0$ (flat start), share one sharpness $f$, or fix $f=0.05$ (sharp). The
anchored forms hold the ceiling at 0.887, the ROC AUC of TabPFN on the same 21 tasks. The two references without a
ceiling are a line in $\log C$ and a bare power law on the gap.

\paragraph{Fitting.}
Gap forms are fitted on $\log g$, the logit power law on $\operatorname{logit}(\text{AUC})$, and all other forms on
ROC AUC. We minimise a Huber loss with $\delta=10^{-3}$ with L-BFGS-B, starting from a grid of initial values (at most
500 for full fits and 100 for holdout refits). Writing compute as $c=\ln(C/10^{13})$ instead of $\ln C$ matters: with
$\ln C\approx30$, amplitude and exponent are nearly collinear, and the optimiser leaves the amplitude at its initial
value. Ceilings must lie at or above the best ROC AUC in the fitted points, and fitted floors 0.05 below it. Two-stage
forms refit every stage on the same points as the final stage.

\paragraph{Two leaks in the evaluation.}
Both leaks are easy to introduce and both favour the wrong forms. First, the ceiling bound must use the best ROC AUC
of the training points, not of the whole frontier. With the frontier's best point (0.872) as the bound, 13 of the 25
forms with a free ceiling put their ceiling exactly on it when refitted on the points up to $3.2\times10^{13}$ FLOPs,
whose best value is 0.806. The bound then does the extrapolation. With the correct bound, the holdout error of the
logistic in $\log C$ rises from 0.005 to 0.010, and that of BNSL with one break from 0.005 to 0.012. Second, holdout
refits must not start from the parameters fitted on all points. A form with more parameters than training points
barely moves from such a start, so it reproduces the test points. With both leaks in place, BNSL with two breaks reached a
long-horizon error of 0.003 on the top-5 frontier. Without them, its fits diverge. Rounds of this
analysis before the fixes reported BNSL with two breaks as the best form, with a ceiling of 0.887.

\paragraph{The ceiling follows the data.}
Table~\ref{tab:ceiling-drift} refits four forms on the points up to each compute level. Below $10^{13.75}$ FLOPs the
ceilings jump around, because the fits rest on few points. Above it they settle into a pattern: each ceiling stays
0.004 to 0.045 above the best ROC AUC observed so far and rises with it. None of the four forms predicts at $10^{14}$ FLOPs
the ceiling it finds at $10^{16.5}$ FLOPs.

\paragraph{Robustness.}
We repeat the evaluation on two variants of the frontier. The first replaces the best run at each level by the mean
of the five best, and drops levels with fewer than 20 runs (21 points, up to $10^{16.25}$ FLOPs). This reduces the
upward bias of taking a maximum over thousands of noisy runs. The logit power law stays the best form with a free
ceiling at long horizons (0.039), next to the power law on the gap (0.040). M4, the Rosenfeld envelope, the step form and
the two-stage form through the real NLL follow at 0.062 to 0.073. The two-stage forms through the synthetic loss and the
loss-to-loss chain fall to 0.12 and 0.13, and BNSL with two breaks diverges. The logit power law puts the ceiling at
0.891 $[0.883, 0.900]$ and the power law on the gap at 0.881 $[0.877, 0.891]$. The second variant keeps the frontier but
weights each point by the inverse of its seed noise. We take the standard deviation of the frontier ROC AUC across four
seeds of the $10^{13}$-FLOP stage (0.015 to 0.063), and keep its last value beyond $10^{14}$ FLOPs, where no seeds were
run. The order of the best forms does not change: logit power law 0.025, two-stage through the real NLL 0.026,
two-stage through the synthetic loss 0.029, power law on the gap 0.032. The logit power law's ceiling stays at 0.896,
and BNSL with two breaks again fails at long horizons (0.23).

\paragraph{The restricted frontier.}
The frontier over the runs with the largest prior has 20 points from 11 runs, 8 of them checkpoints of a single run.
On it, every form fails at long horizons (MAE 0.13 or more) and at medium horizons (0.04 or more). Refitted on the points
up to $3.2\times10^{13}$ FLOPs, the best form, a two-break BNSL variant, still errs by 0.044, and half of those test
points come from runs that are also in the training set. Most forms put the ceiling at the best observed point, 0.866,
and their profiles do not bound it. Only on this frontier do multi-break forms have the lowest holdout error at $3.2\times10^{13}$ FLOPs, which is how
an earlier version of this analysis came to favour them.
